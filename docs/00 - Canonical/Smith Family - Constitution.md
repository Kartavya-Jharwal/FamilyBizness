# Smith Family Constitution

## Preamble

We steward our family enterprise with craftsmanship, modest living, continuity and personal responsibility. Our [canonical family identity](Identity.md#smith-family--hypothetical-reference-family) records our hypothetical heritage. We use a private, proportionate wealth management platform to coordinate our affairs and preserve family oversight as the business evolves. External advisers support specific needs without replacing that oversight.

This newly drafted constitution synthesises the supplied source material into one Smith family policy. It replaces the combined operating charter. It is a fictional prototype specification, not an executed legal instrument. GBP and the project’s English-law baseline apply. Existing numerical controls are prototype policy choices, not historical family facts. The investment mandate and financial scenario remain separate records.

## Article 1 — Cash, reserves and modest distributions

**Trace: FO-CASH.** Before distributions, fund twelve months of debt and statutory obligations, six months of fixed operating costs and the approved family-support reserve. Confirm solvency, covenant compliance, required operating compliance and available distributable profits. Allocate remaining cash 50% to reinvestment and reserves, 30% to permitted dividends and 20% to ring-fenced growth. These percentages apply to remaining cash, not total wealth. Personal consumption must not compromise continuity.

## Article 2 — Decision rights and conflicts

**Trace: FO-VOTES.** Reserved decisions require 75% of eligible disinterested owner voting rights and a two-thirds Board recommendation. Quorum requires 50% of eligible voters by headcount and representation outside the largest branch where available. Declare conflicts and exclude conflicted rights from the eligible denominator. Record separate company, trust and contractual approvals; family agreement alone does not confer legal authority.

## Article 3 — Ownership and beneficiaries

**Trace: FO-OWNERS.** Maintain separate records for registered ownership, beneficial interests, family membership and appointed authority. Beneficiary status creates no automatic shareholder vote, employment entitlement or power to act as trustee. Keep the prototype ownership and financial scenario separate from the family history; no scenario amount establishes actual Smith family wealth.

## Article 4 — Proportionate family governance

**Trace: FO-GOVERNANCE.** The Family Assembly meets annually, the Council monthly, owners at least twice yearly and the Board quarterly. The prototype uses five Council members and five directors, including two independent directors, with a professional CEO. These are governance roles, not a requirement for a large salaried family office. Management handles delegated operations; the Council handles family matters; the Board oversees strategy and appointments; owners decide reserved ownership matters.

## Article 5 — Business continuity and ownership review

**Trace: FO-EXIT.** Review whether continued ownership best serves the family, employees and enduring enterprise. The prototype uses 2028–2030 as a planning review window, not a promised sale. Prepare independent valuation, debt and tax analysis, employee transition and equal information for minority owners. Maintain leadership succession and key-person handover if the business is retained. Sale proceeds are never assumed before completion.

## Article 6 — Orderly ownership liquidity

**Trace: FO-TRANSFER.** Offer proposed transfers first to the seller’s branch for 21 days, then other eligible owners for 21 days, then the company for 60 days. An approved employee trust may be considered for 30 days only if it exists. Review affordable liquidity of up to 5% of issued shares every three years; the initial prototype review is 2027. Obtain independent valuation, avoid minority discounts in compulsory-transfer review and document funding, signed terms and competent approvals. No buy-back or transfer is guaranteed.

## Article 7 — Merit and family employment

**Trace: FO-EMPLOYMENT.** Family management candidates need relevant qualifications, three years of external experience and successful independent assessment for an actual vacancy. Apply market pay, ordinary performance standards and non-family review. Avoid direct reporting to close relatives. Family membership does not guarantee a job, promotion or leadership appointment.

## Article 8 — Craftsmanship and the next generation

**Trace: FO-DEVELOPMENT.** Teach craftsmanship, financial literacy, ownership responsibilities and sound judgement. Provide mentoring across family branches and business functions, six-month rotations where practical and periodic development reviews. Non-voting observation prepares future stewards without transferring ownership or executive authority.

## Article 9 — Respectful dispute resolution

**Trace: FO-DISPUTES.** Seek confidential direct discussion within 14 days, followed by disinterested Council review and independent mediation where needed. Record conflicts, responsible participants and next actions. Preserve access to urgent protection and remedies under applicable instruments. No unresolved disagreement should be disguised as family consensus.

## Article 10 — Information and accountability

**Trace: FO-COMMS.** Circulate meeting papers seven days before meetings and minutes within ten days afterwards. Aim to notify entitled owners of material news within 48 hours of the event. Provide quarterly owner reporting and distinguish family, owner, Board and adviser information. Protect privacy and record decisions; prototype visitor profiles are not security permissions.

## Article 11 — Heritage, community and responsible investment

**Trace: FO-LEGACY.** Preserve the craftsmanship, modest living, continuity and personal responsibility described in the canonical family identity. Support education, apprenticeships and community wellbeing through affordable, reviewed grants. Record diligence and approval before commitments. The Board maintains a separate investment mandate consistent with stewardship; no giving entitlement or guaranteed return arises from this constitution.

## Article 12 — Estate and emergency continuity

**Trace: FO-CONTINUITY.** Review wills, powers of attorney, trust instruments, nominations, insurance and deputy mandates every three years and after material events. Keep legal ownership, beneficial interests, authority and funding timing distinct. Identify interim leadership and succession liquidity. Use qualified advisers for instruments and retain evidence of appointments and approvals.

## Article 13 — Review, amendments and implementation

**Trace: FO-INSTRUMENTS.** Review this constitution every three years and after death, incapacity, ownership change, major disputes or material legal changes. Record proposed changes, affected rights, owner decisions and required instrument approvals. Apply the reserved-decision process and obtain affected-owner consent before reducing protected individual rights. Keep previous revisions and invalidate dependent checks when adopted clauses change. Route corrections through the recorded please fix workflow. This is a hypothetical prototype constitution, not evidence of legal execution or an actual family vote.
